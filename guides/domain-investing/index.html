Domain Investing 101

Domain investing is the practice of buying domain names you believe will be valuable later — then selling them to a business, builder, or another investor. Here's how to start without burning money on names nobody wants.

What domain investing actually is

A domain name is digital real estate. Just like land, some addresses are far more valuable than others because of location (the right keywords), how memorable they are, and how many people want them. Investors register or buy these names and resell them at a markup — sometimes for a few hundred dollars, occasionally for five or six figures.

Mindful note: domain investing is a real skill, not a lottery ticket. Most flips are modest. Treat it like a patient side portfolio, not a get-rich-quick scheme.

What makes a domain valuable

  • Short and memorable — easy to say, type, and remember.
  • Keyword relevance — names that match what buyers search for or sell.
  • Extension (TLD) — .com still leads; newer TLDs are riskier.
  • Brandability — does it sound like a real company?
  • No trademark conflicts — never buy someone else's brand name.

How to research and value names

  1. Check availability & price at a registrar (Namecheap, GoDaddy, Porkbun).
  2. Look at comparable sales on marketplaces like Sedo, Afternic, and Dan.com to see what similar names actually sold for.
  3. Gauge demand — is there an industry or trend that needs this word?
  4. Estimate, don't guess — automated appraisal tools are a starting point, not gospel.

Where to buy and sell

  • Register fresh names at a registrar for ~$10–15/year.
  • Buy aftermarket names on Sedo, Afternic, Dan.com, or at expiry auctions (GoDaddy Auctions, DropCatch).
  • List for sale across multiple marketplaces and add a "for sale" landing page on the domain itself.

A simple starter strategy

  1. Set a small annual budget you're comfortable losing.
  2. Pick one niche you understand (local services, a hobby, an emerging tech term).
  3. Hand-register 5–10 strong, brandable .com names in that niche.
  4. List them for sale and renew only the names getting inquiries.
  5. Reinvest your wins; let go of names that get no interest after a year.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Overpaying for trendy junk that won't age well.
  • Hoarding hundreds of names you'll never sell (renewals add up fast).
  • Registering trademarked terms — that's a legal headache, not an asset.
  • Expecting instant sales. Good domains can take months or years to move.
