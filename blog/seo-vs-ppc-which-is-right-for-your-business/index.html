SEO vs. PPC: Which Is Right for Your Business?

SEO and PPC both get you found on Google, but they work in almost opposite ways. Understanding the difference — not just in theory, but in cost, timeline, and effort — makes it much easier to decide where to put your first marketing dollar.

What SEO Actually Is

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the practice of improving your website so it ranks higher in Google's organic (unpaid) search results. It covers technical site health, on-page content, local signals like your Google Business Profile, and earning links and citations from other sites. The traffic it earns is "free" in the sense that you don't pay per click — but it takes real time and consistent work to build.

What PPC Actually Is

Pay-per-click (PPC) advertising — Google Ads, Meta Ads, and similar platforms — puts your business directly in front of people searching or browsing, and you pay each time someone clicks. Results are close to immediate: launch a campaign today, get traffic today. The trade-off is that traffic stops the moment you stop paying.

Cost Comparison

SEO's cost is mostly labor and time — content, technical fixes, and ongoing optimization — typically billed as a monthly retainer. PPC's cost is a combination of agency management fees plus actual ad spend paid directly to the platform, which scales with how much traffic you want. SEO tends to get cheaper per lead over time as rankings build; PPC costs stay roughly proportional to the traffic you're buying.

Timeline Comparison

  • PPC: traffic within hours of launching a campaign
  • SEO: meaningful ranking movement typically takes 3–6 months, longer in competitive industries or markets

When to Choose PPC First

  • You need leads or sales now — a new location opening, a seasonal push, a launch
  • You want to test messaging or offers quickly before investing in long-term content
  • Your industry is too competitive to realistically rank organically in the short term

When to Choose SEO First

  • You're building for the long term and want compounding, lower-cost-per-lead traffic
  • Your budget can't sustain ongoing ad spend indefinitely
  • Your industry has strong, consistent search demand that doesn't require urgency

Why Most Businesses End Up Doing Both

PPC can fund growth while SEO is still building momentum in the background, and SEO reduces long-term reliance on paid traffic once it kicks in. Many businesses run PPC early for immediate volume, then shift budget toward SEO as organic rankings mature and the cost-per-lead from search starts to drop.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just do SEO and skip PPC entirely?

Yes, plenty of businesses do — it just means accepting a slower ramp-up period before organic traffic becomes meaningful.

Does running PPC help my SEO rankings?

Not directly — Google doesn't use ad spend as a ranking factor for organic results. Indirectly, PPC can surface which keywords and pages convert, which can inform your SEO content strategy.

Which is better for a brand-new business with no website history?

PPC usually makes more sense early on, since a new site has no ranking history or authority yet — SEO can be built in parallel.
