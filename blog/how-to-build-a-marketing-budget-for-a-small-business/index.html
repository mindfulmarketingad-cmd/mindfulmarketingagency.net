How to Build a Marketing Budget for a Small Business

Building a marketing budget from scratch is harder than it sounds — too little and nothing moves, too much in the wrong place and you've wasted money you can't easily get back. Here's a practical framework to work from.

On this page

  2. Separate Fixed Costs From Variable Spend
  3. Allocate Across Channels Based on Goals, Not Trends
  4. Track Cost Per Lead and Cost Per Customer
  5. Revisit the Budget Quarterly
  6. Common Budgeting Mistakes

Start With a Revenue Benchmark

A widely used starting point is allocating roughly 5–10% of revenue to marketing for an established business, with newer or growth-focused businesses sometimes allocating more to build initial visibility faster. Treat this as a starting range to adjust, not a fixed rule.

Separate Fixed Costs From Variable Spend

  • Fixed costs — retainers, software/tool subscriptions, website hosting
  • Variable spend — ad spend, which can flex up or down month to month based on performance and cash flow

Separating the two makes it much easier to see what you can scale back in a lean month without losing core capability.

Allocate Across Channels Based on Goals, Not Trends

Rather than copying what's trendy, allocate based on your specific goal: SEO and content for long-term, compounding visibility; PPC for immediate, controllable volume; social media for brand and community; email for retention and repeat business. Most small businesses do best concentrating budget in one or two channels well rather than spreading thin across all of them.

Track Cost Per Lead and Cost Per Customer

A budget without tracking is just a guess. At minimum, track how many leads and customers each channel actually generates relative to what you spent on it — this is what lets you shift budget toward what's working and away from what isn't.

Revisit the Budget Quarterly

Marketing performance shifts with seasonality, competition, and your own growth stage. A quarterly check-in — not just an annual one — keeps the budget responsive instead of locked to assumptions made a year ago.

Common Budgeting Mistakes

  • Spreading a small budget across too many channels, so none of them get enough to work
  • Cutting marketing entirely during a slow month, which often deepens the slowdown
  • No tracking, so spend can't be tied to actual results
  • Chasing trends instead of the channel that fits the actual business and audience

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of revenue should a new business spend on marketing?

Newer businesses often need to spend above the typical 5–10% benchmark early on to build initial visibility, then can taper toward the standard range as the business matures.

Should I include staff time in my marketing budget?

Yes — the time spent by you or your team on marketing has a real cost, even if it isn't a direct cash expense, and should factor into whether outsourcing makes financial sense.
