What If My Business Domain Is Already Taken?

You've settled on a business name, you're ready to build a website, and then it happens — the domain is already registered. It's one of the most common snags in starting or rebranding a business, and it's rarely the dead end it feels like at first.

Here's how to work through it methodically, starting with the cheapest options first.

First, Check What's Actually There

Before changing plans, find out what you're dealing with. Visit the domain directly:

  • It's an active, unrelated business. You'll likely need a different name or extension — see the options below.
  • It's a parked page or "domain for sale" notice. The owner may be a domain investor willing to sell — see "Buy the domain" below.
  • It shows nothing at all. It may still be registered but unused. A WHOIS lookup (through your registrar or a site like whois.com) can confirm registration status and, sometimes, ownership contact details.

Option 1: Try a Different Extension

.com is still the most recognized and trusted extension for most businesses, but it's no longer the only credible option. Depending on your industry, consider:

  • .co — a popular, clean-looking .com alternative
  • .net or .biz — widely recognized, if slightly dated-feeling
  • Industry-specific extensions — .agency, .law, .shop, .tech, and similar options can actually reinforce what you do
  • .us — a reasonable option for a business specifically serving U.S. customers

Search ranking is not meaningfully affected by which extension you choose — Google ranks content and authority, not TLD. The main trade-off is customer trust and the risk that people will type ".com" out of habit and land on a competitor's site instead.

Option 2: Add a Word or Modifier

Small additions can make an otherwise-taken name available again without changing your actual business name:

  • A location: yourbrandatlanta.com
  • A descriptor: yourbrandmarketing.com, yourbrandhq.com
  • An action word: getyourbrand.com, tryyourbrand.com, shopyourbrand.com

This keeps your actual brand name unchanged on signage, social handles, and verbal introductions — only the domain gets a small addition.

Option 3: Buy the Domain From Its Current Owner

If the domain is dormant, parked, or clearly held by a domain investor rather than a competing business, you can often buy it outright. Steps:

  • Look up the registrant through a WHOIS lookup, or check if the registrar offers a "make an offer" or "buy now" option directly on the parked page.
  • Reach out professionally and make a reasonable offer — premium or aftermarket domains can range from a few hundred to tens of thousands of dollars depending on demand.
  • Use an escrow service (such as Escrow.com) for the transaction rather than wiring money directly — this protects both buyer and seller until the domain transfer is confirmed.

Be prepared to walk away if the asking price doesn't make sense for your budget — there's almost always a workable alternative.

Option 4: Adjust the Name Itself

Sometimes the cleanest fix is a small tweak to the business name itself — a synonym, a different spelling, or dropping a generic word — that happens to free up a clean, close-match domain. This is worth considering early, before the name is on signage, packaging, or legal paperwork, since changing it later gets expensive fast.

Option 5: Use a Domain Marketplace or Broker

If the name is listed for sale, marketplaces like Afternic, Sedo, and GoDaddy's aftermarket let you make an offer or buy at a listed price, often with built-in escrow and transfer handling. A domain broker can also negotiate on your behalf for premium or high-value names, usually for a commission on the sale.

What Not to Do

  • Don't register a near-identical domain to an existing trademarked brand — this can trigger a legal dispute and you could lose the domain (and more) regardless of intent.
  • Don't ignore the problem and launch on a confusing workaround (like a free subdomain) if you're a serious business — it undercuts trust from day one.
  • Don't overpay out of urgency. A few days of research almost always turns up a workable, affordable option.

Frequently asked questions

Does the domain extension affect SEO rankings?

Not directly. Google ranks based on content quality, relevance, and authority — not the TLD. The bigger consideration is user trust and whether people will remember and correctly type your domain.

Is it safe to buy a domain directly from a stranger?

Use an escrow service rather than sending payment directly. Reputable escrow providers hold the funds until the domain transfer is verified, protecting both sides of the transaction.

Should I just use a free subdomain instead?

For a serious, ongoing business, a proper domain is worth the investment — it's more memorable, more trustworthy to customers, and won't need to change later as you grow.
